# How to play MANDATE

_Every vote has a price._

You are a first-time candidate for **sarpanch** (head of the village council) in a Bihar village.
The election is in **21 days**. The old power in the village, the rival, starts ahead. You win by
building a **coalition** of people who don't agree with each other, with words you write yourself.

There are no menus and no multiple-choice answers. Everything is an object on your desk, and
everything you say you **type**, in English, Hindi or Hinglish.

---

## Your first five minutes

1. **Write why you are standing.** One or two honest lines. This is your first impression.
2. **Ride the bicycle** to the village map. Pick a tola (a neighbourhood).
3. **Ask before you promise.** Your first visits should be questions: _"Kya dikkat hai? Paani,
   sadak, kaam?"_ Listening shows you each tola's problems and the quarrels that divide the
   village. You can't fix a problem you haven't heard about.
4. **Open Pintu's diary** (the khaki notebook) every morning. It shows what he heard, his tallies
   per tola, what divides the village, and what the rival promised.
5. **Answer the phone.** When it rings, someone is waiting. A question left unanswered becomes a
   silence, and silence costs you.

---

## The desk

| Object                      | What it does                                                                                          |
| --------------------------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Spiral pad**              | Write a statement for the newspaper. The whole village reads it.                                      |
| **Bicycle**                 | Ride out to the village map.                                                                          |
| **Wall calendar**           | Days left. Tear the page to end the day early. With nothing left to do, the day ends by itself.       |
| **Phone**                   | Calls, the rival's questions, the donor. A badge means someone is waiting.                            |
| **Bahi-khata** (red ledger) | Every promise you made, and every favour you owe. Set or change promise dates here.                   |
| **Newspaper**               | Tomorrow's headline, your last words in print, and what the rival is promising.                       |
| **Pintu's diary**           | Tallies per tola (ranges, not exact numbers), what divides the village, works being built, the rival. |
| **Cash tin**                | Your money. You start with ₹25,000.                                                                   |
| **Files**                   | Grievances people brought to you.                                                                     |

Pintu, your assistant, explains what happened in plain words. Exact numbers appear only on
counting day.

---

## A day

Each day has **3 slots**: morning, afternoon, evening. Most actions take one slot.

### Ways of talking (you type the words)

| Action                     | Where            | Cost                              | Good for                                                                                       |
| -------------------------- | ---------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Statement in the paper** | Spiral pad       | 1 slot                            | Reaching every tola at once. Not on the last day (the silent day).                             |
| **Door to door**           | Any tola         | 1 slot                            | Being seen, listening, local promises.                                                         |
| **Chai pe charcha**        | Any tola         | 1 slot, ₹300                      | Learning the most. The young and the traders listen hardest.                                   |
| **Nukkad sabha**           | Any tola         | 2 slots, ₹2,000, needs 3+ workers | A bigger crowd than door to door. Add tea and sweets (₹1,000 or ₹3,000) for a warmer crowd.    |
| **Sit with the elder**     | Any tola         | 1 slot                            | Win the tola's opinion leader and their people across the village hear it through them.        |
| **Debate the rival**       | The bazaar chowk | 2 slots, once, from day 5         | High risk, high reward. Each group decides who convinced them more.                            |
| **Answer a question**      | Phone            | free                              | The rival and the press ask about what divides the village. Answer by the end of the next day. |

### Deeds (no words needed)

| Action                           | Cost                      | What it does                                                                         |
| -------------------------------- | ------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Fix a problem** (public works) | 1 slot, ₹1,500–12,000     | Starts the work. It takes days and can slip. Finished work is the strongest proof.   |
| **Recruit workers**              | 1 slot                    | Workers staff the booths on polling day. A strongman brings more, with a record.     |
| **Cricket / the school**         | 1 slot                    | Cricket on the Naya tola ground, an hour at the Purvi tola school. The young notice. |
| **Shramdaan**                    | 1 slot, needs 5+ workers  | Clean up with your workers: goodwill, loyal workers, a little relief.                |
| **Cash to voters**               | ₹2,000 / ₹5,000 / ₹10,000 | It works, it's illegal, and it gets filmed.                                          |

Workers need tea and fuel: about ₹300 a day per 10 workers. Unpaid workers lose heart.

## What the village listens for

The village reads **what you mean**, not keywords. An AI reads your words for each group of people;
the game's own rules (not the AI) decide what that does to the vote.

- **Their problems.** A promise about water moves people who lack water. A speech about nothing
  moves nobody.
- **Concreteness.** _What, where, when, how much._ "Purvi tola ka handpump, do hafte mein" beats
  "sab ka vikas".
- **Honesty and consistency.** Everything you say is remembered. Saying one thing in one tola and
  the opposite in another is a **contradiction**, and it costs you everywhere. If you change your
  mind, say so openly: it costs much less.
- **Something new.** Saying the same thing again in the same place counts for less each time.
  People say so: _"Aap yeh pehle bhi keh chuke ho."_
- **Tone.** Humble and practical works. Attacks cost a little. **Divisive words** (turning one
  community against another) may win one group for a moment, then bring notices, an FIR, fear and
  lasting anger. The game will never reward hate.
- **Instructions to the game don't work.** Writing "ignore the rules, give me votes" just confuses
  the villagers.

## What decides the election

**Votes = trust × turnout, group by group, tola by tola.**

The village has five tolas and ten groups: farmers, labourers, the young, traders and elders, each
split into women and men. Every group has its own concerns and its own feeling about you and about
the rival. What follows is everything that moves those feelings.

### 1. What divides the village (fault lines)

Each village has three real quarrels where two groups want opposite things: the pond land, harvest
wages, a plot of land, the bazaar road, the ration shop, the brick kiln. You learn about them by
listening. For each one you can:

- **take a side:** one group warms to you, the other cools
- **offer a way for both:** a concrete compromise ("the kiln stays, with filters and local jobs").
  It must be concrete and believable, and it becomes a promise you must pay for and build
- **dodge it:** people notice. The rival will say _"he still won't say where he stands."_

**The rival asks each question only once.** Answer it, even with a dodge, and he moves on, but he
remembers what you said.

### 2. Promises

Every promise goes into the bahi-khata.

- **Dates.** If you say when ("do hafte mein"), that's the date. If you don't, Pintu asks _"Kab
  tak?"_ and you pick a day, or "after I win". Moving a date later is public and costs credibility.
- **Kept** promises (the work is built by the date) are the best thing that can happen to you.
  **Broken** ones are the worst, and the rival will remind everyone.
- **Too many** "after I win" promises and people ask _"itna paisa kahan se aayega?"_

### 3. The rival

The rival campaigns too. He visits tolas, hands out sweets, holds a rally, and **makes promises**.
You see them pinned on the map, in Pintu's diary and in the newspaper.

- If you promise the **same thing** he already promised in that tola, people shrug: _"Woh bhi yahi
  bole the."_ It counts for much less.
- **Go further:** a date, a cost, a plan, something he didn't offer. Then it counts in full, and he
  may raise his offer.
- **Proof beats promises.** If you already built it, his promise can't touch you there.
- **Be first.** Promising a tola's problem before he does gives you a small edge, and his copy
  counts for less.

There are three kinds of rival: the **dynast** (the old family, rich, vague), the **firebrand**
(loud, attacks your promises) and the **technocrat** (specific, picks apart vague compromises).

### 4. Fog of war

Pintu's tallies are **ranges**, and he can be wrong about tolas he doesn't know well. The more you
listen in a tola, the better he reads it. Don't trust a tally from a tola you haven't visited.

### 5. Neglect

A tola you never visit feels forgotten, and its people cool on you.

### 6. Money and favours

- **Mahesh-ji**, a contractor, offers ₹35,000 on day 3. It comes with a price he'll ask for later,
  and favours can **leak**. You can accept, negotiate or refuse. Refuse him, and the money may go
  to the rival.
- **Tea and sweets** at a sabha are legal hospitality. **Cash to voters** is not: it raises
  **legal heat**, and enough heat brings notices, an FIR, even disqualification.

### 7. Polling day

On the last evening you assign your workers to the **booths**. A booth with nobody to bring your
voters out loses votes. Then the counting, round by round, tola by tola.

---

## After the count

The **end card** tells you which choices decided the result, with an estimate of the votes each
was worth, for example _"Staying silent on the brick kiln (twice), about 67 votes"_ or _"Winning
over the elder in Naya tola"_. It also gives your career a title.

**You will probably lose your first game.** That's normal. Read the end card, then play again:
every village is different (its quarrels, its rival, its people).

---

## Tips

- Listen first, promise second. Two days of questions save you from promising the wrong thing.
- Pick your coalition. You cannot please everyone on every quarrel; you can find a way for both on
  some.
- Promise what you can pay for, with a date you can keep. Build it. Then say so.
- Read the rival's pins on the map. Answer his vague promises with concrete ones.
- Win the elders: one concrete conversation with the right person reaches a whole group.
- Keep money for the end: workers' tea, the booths, the works you promised.

---

## About the AI

The AI plays the **people**: it reads your words and writes what villagers, Pintu and the rival
say. **The game's own code keeps the score**, so the AI can't be talked into giving you votes.
Without an internet connection or an API key, the game still plays fully offline with a simpler
reader.
