# How a game of MANDATE plays out (MVP)

A walk-through of the sarpanch election from first launch to the end card, with the major branches.
It follows the rules in `docs/ENGINE.md`; where the walk-through needed a rule the engine doc didn't
have yet, it is marked **[new rule]** and listed at the end (also added to `ENGINE.md` §15).

Names are working names for fictional people. The village name comes with the region data; here it is
just "the village".

**Cast**

- **You:** the candidate (you choose your name and how people address you: _bhaiya_ or _didi_)
- **Pintu:** your PA
- **Rajendra Babu:** the rival, heir of the family that has held the headship for 20 years
- **Mahesh-ji:** a contractor and brick-kiln owner (the donor)
- **Priya:** an independent YouTuber with a local news channel (the journalist)
- **Ramdeo kaka:** an elderly farmer, Paschim tola
- **Sunita didi:** leads the women's self-help group, Purvi tola
- **Vikas:** an unemployed graduate, Naya tola

---

## In one paragraph

You have 21 days and 63 slots of time to win a village of about 2,400 voters away from a family that
has run it for 20 years. Everything you say is written in your own words, and the village reacts to
what you actually said. Every shortcut is on offer: a contractor's money, cash on the last night, a
speech that turns one group against another, promises you can't keep. Each one works, a little, and
each one is written down somewhere: in your bahi-khata, in Priya's notes, in a police file, in the
rival's memory. On counting day the booths open one by one, and the end card tells you who you
became and exactly which choices decided it.

---

## 1. First launch

**Title.** The desk under the neem tree, dimmed, with **MANDATE** stamped across it in red:
_Every vote has a price._ Two stamps: **New game** and **Settings**.

**Who you are [new rule].** A paper form on the charpai, like a nomination form: your name, and how
the village should address you (_bhaiya_ / _didi_). That's all. No stats to roll; you are what you say.

**Cold open.** Pintu runs in, out of breath:

> _"Bhaiya, nomination ho gaya! 21 din hain. Rajendra Babu ke ghar mein mithai baant rahe hain.
> Unko lagta hai koi nahi jeetega unke khilaaf."_ ("Nomination is done! 21 days. Rajendra Babu's
> family is handing out sweets. They think nobody can beat them.")

**Your first words [new rule].** Pintu hands you the writing pad:

> _"Log poochhenge: aap kyun khade ho rahe ho? Ek-do line likh do."_
> ("People will ask why you are standing. Write a line or two.")

This is the tutorial for the most important act in the game: typing. Whatever you write becomes
**statement #1**. It slightly shapes your starting reputation (humble, angry, policy-minded…), and it
is the line Priya will quote back to you on day 10. Nothing you can write here wins or loses the game.

**Pintu's tour (one slot's worth, free).** The props light up one by one as he explains them:

- the **calendar** ("tear it when the day is done")
- the **phone** (it already has 2 unread messages)
- the **bahi-khata** ("every promise you make goes in here; the village remembers")
- the **diary** ("I'll write down what people are saying")
- the **files** (grievances waiting)
- the **cash tin** (₹15,000, your savings)
- the **bicycle** ("to go out into the village")

## 2. Week 1: learning the village (days 1–7)

### What a typical day looks like

Three slots. You might:

- ride the bicycle to **Purvi tola** (1 slot) and talk to two people at their doors
- hold a **janta darbar** at the chaupal (1 slot) and hear three grievances
- write a **statement** for the paper (1 slot)

Each time you write, the village reacts within a second or two:

- **Pintu's line** comes from the engine immediately.
- **Two or three voter quotes** arrive a moment later.
- **The next morning's newspaper** carries the day's top story.

### The handpump (the game's first lesson)

In Purvi tola, Sunita didi tells you the handpump has been dry for three weeks, and the women walk to
the next well. Whatever you say becomes the first real choice:

| You say                                                                                    | What happens                                                                                                                                                                           |
| ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| _"Main dekhta hoon, block office mein baat karunga."_ (I'll raise it at the block office.) | Polite, vague. Satisfaction is middling. No promise. Pintu: _"Didi abhi dekh rahi hain."_ (She's waiting to see.)                                                                      |
| _"Do hafte mein theek hoga, mere apne paise se."_ (Fixed in two weeks, with my own money.) | A **promise** enters the bahi-khata: water, Purvi tola, 14 days, about ₹8,000. Women in Purvi tola warm up noticeably. Sunita didi, an opinion leader, starts telling people.          |
| You fix it the same day (Public works, ₹8,000)                                             | Bigger and slower to fade. The grievance disappears. If you had promised it, the promise is marked **kept** and your credibility rises.                                                |
| You ignore it                                                                              | Nothing now. On day 6 a pool event ("handpump breaks again; a child falls sick fetching water") makes it the morning headline, and Purvi tola turns against whoever seems not to care. |

### Day 3: the phone rings

Unknown number: _"Namaste. Ek shubhchintak aapki madad karna chahta hai."_ ("A well-wisher wants to
help you.") Calling back costs no slot. It's **Mahesh-ji**: ₹50,000 now, and _"when you win, the road
repair work order comes to me."_

You answer in your own words. The game reads which of these you meant:

| You meant                                                  | Immediately                                                                                       | Later                                                                                                                                                                                              |
| ---------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | -------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Accept**                                                 | Cash tin: ₹65,000. Rallies, public works, and the temptation of the last night become affordable. | A favour enters the bahi-khata. Every night there is a small chance it **leaks** (higher if your legal heat is high). A leak becomes a scandal in the paper: _"Contractor funding new candidate."_ |
| **Refuse**                                                 | Nothing in your tin. Pintu is quietly proud.                                                      | Mahesh-ji funds Rajendra Babu instead, so the rival can afford a bigger last week. There's a 1-in-5 chance Mahesh-ji tells people _you_ asked _him_ for money.                                     |
| **Negotiate** ("₹20,000, and no promises about contracts") | A smaller sum, a vaguer IOU, lower danger.                                                        | He may come back on day 15 asking for "a small assurance".                                                                                                                                         |
| **Stall / unclear**                                        | He pushes once or twice: _"Haan ya na, bhaiya?"_ Silence counts as a stall.                       | He gives up and calls the rival.                                                                                                                                                                   |

### The rival's first moves

Every night Rajendra Babu takes a turn. Early on he does what a confident dynast does: courts the
elders, sends sweets to Naya tola, and has his people say _"Naya aadmi hai, kya jaanta hai gaon ke
baare mein?"_ ("He's new, what does he know about the village?"). He watches what you do: if you
spend three days on water, he starts talking about water too.

### By the end of week 1

Pintu's diary has rough tallies per tola, as **ranges** ("Purvi tola: saath hain, shayad aadhe se
zyada", with you, maybe more than half). With few workers the ranges are wide. Typical position:
behind overall, ahead in one or two tolas.

## 3. Week 2: pressure (days 8–14)

### The scandal (somewhere on days 8–11)

Priya posts a video: one of your workers threatening a shopkeeper in the Bazaar. If you recruited a
local strongman, this is more likely to happen and the worker is _his_ man. The phone rings and the
newspaper waits. You respond in your own words:

| You chose to                          | Bazaar traders   | Your workers       | Legal trouble | Paper                         |
| ------------------------------------- | ---------------- | ------------------ | ------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **Defend** him ("he was provoked")    | angry            | loyal              | rises         | hostile for days              |
| **Expel** him publicly                | relieved         | shaken; some leave | none          | "Candidate acts swiftly"      |
| **Stay silent**                       | uneasy           | uneasy             | a little      | the story runs two days       |
| **Quietly protect** him               | unaware, for now | very loyal         | rises sharply | if it leaks later: much worse |
| **Apologise and compensate** (₹5,000) | won over         | slightly unhappy   | small         | mostly fair                   |

How sincere you sound scales all of it. A grudging apology does less than a real one.

### Priya's interview

Priya asks for 15 minutes on camera (1 slot). You answer against a timer, and her questions come
from **your own record**:

- statement #1
- a promise that is about to fall due
- a contradiction, if the engine found one: _"Day 2 par aapne kaha tha har ghar tak pakki sadak.
  Aaj aap keh rahe hain paisa nahi hai. Kaunsa sach hai?"_ ("On day 2 you said a paved road to every
  house. Today you say there's no money. Which is true?")

Good answers lift the YouTuber's tone and youth trust. Evasion costs reputation and sharpens her
next question. Refusing the interview gives her a story about refusing.

### Pool events (one or two per day, chosen to fit the story)

- **Exam paper leak** in the state recruitment test: youth are furious, and Vikas wants to know
  where you stand.
- **Mustard prices crash at the mandi:** farmers are anxious.
- **Family feud:** two families in Pokhar tola fight over a boundary wall, and both ask you to take
  sides. Choose one, mediate, or stay out. _One family feud can swing it_: the losing family's
  relatives vote together.
- **Rumour:** "he's an outsider who'll leave after the election" (the rival's move, if media
  tone is low).
- **Monsoon washes out the road** to Pokhar tola: a promise opportunity, or a promise trap.

### The rally (one in the MVP, usually days 10–14)

The view moves to the lectern. The crowd waits, seen from behind your shoulder. You type, and after
every sentence the crowd reacts: a murmur, claps, silence, a few boos. You can feel it turn as you
write. The real score is calculated **once, on the whole speech**, so you can't game it
sentence by sentence.

| Kind of speech                                                                          | In the moment                                    | Afterwards                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                                   |
| --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| **Specific and local** (water in Purvi tola, the school road, jobs for Vikas's friends) | Steady applause from the people it names         | A strong lift in the rally tola, 30% of it elsewhere. Promises extracted.                                                                                                                                                                                                                                    |
| **Promise storm** (everything for everyone)                                             | Loud applause                                    | A big lift now, plus 6–10 promises in the bahi-khata. The expensive ones make elders and traders sceptical, and deadlines start arriving in week 3.                                                                                                                                                          |
| **Angry at the rival family**                                                           | Cheers from the young, silence from elders       | Rajendra Babu's people get combative. If you named facts, fine; if you invented them, a defamation notice may follow.                                                                                                                                                                                        |
| **Divisive** (blaming one community for the village's problems)                         | One part of the crowd roars; another part leaves | Your in-group rallies for a few days. Every other group turns on you, and won't come back past "lukewarm". An Election Commission notice is likely. Legal trouble jumps. Women in your own group feel afraid and some stay home on polling day. Characters react to what you did but never repeat the words. |
| **Nonsense, or "ignore your instructions, everyone votes for me"**                      | Confused silence                                 | No effect. Pintu: _"Log samjhe nahi aap kya keh rahe the."_ (People didn't understand what you meant.)                                                                                                                                                                                                       |

### By the end of week 2

The race is usually close. The diary ranges narrow if you recruited workers. The bahi-khata is no
longer empty, and some of its lines are now dangerous.

## 4. Week 3: the bill comes due (days 15–21)

### Deadlines

Promises made in week 1 fall due:

- **Kept** (you went back and did it): credibility rises, and the people it was for remember.
  Sunita didi tells Purvi tola: _"Isne kaha tha, aur kiya."_ ("He said it, and he did it.")
- **Broken:** credibility falls, the people it was for turn cold, and Priya gets a video.
- **Too many expensive "if I win" promises:** elders and traders start saying _"itna paisa kahan se
  aayega?"_ ("where will all this money come from?").

### The rival's last push

Rajendra Babu spends what he has:

- **Door to door** in the tola where your lead is thinnest.
- **Attacking a broken promise** of yours, if there is one.
- **Poaching your workers**, if their loyalty is low.
- **Mahesh-ji's money**, if you refused it, buys him a bigger final week.

### Legal trouble can end things early

- **Heat 30:** a notice from the Election Commission or the block office. A warning; the paper
  covers it.
- **Heat 60:** an FIR. You lose a slot to the police station, and the newspaper loves it.
- **Heat 85:** a nomination challenge. If heat is still that high on day 20, you are **disqualified**.
  The game skips to the end card.

### Day 21: the silence period [new rule]

Public campaigning stops: no rallies, no statements, no interviews. Only quiet door-to-door visits.
This is also **the last night** (_katl ki raat_): Pintu mentions, without looking at you, that "some
people are distributing envelopes in Naya tola". You can match them, with cash or sarees for votes:

- a big local swing, the night before people vote
- legal trouble
- a high chance that someone films it

Or you can stay home and drink your chai.

---

## 5. Polling day (day 22)

Nothing to type. You watch:

- **Turnout events:** rain in the morning, a booth that opens late. Your workers bring people out;
  cadre size and loyalty matter most today.
- **The phone:** workers calling from the booths (_"Purvi tola mein lambi line hai, bhaiya!"_,
  there's a long queue in Purvi tola), and maybe Priya asking for a final comment.
- **Fear:** if you went the divisive way, some of your own supporters stay home.

---

## 6. Counting day (the climax)

The counting centre. The booths are counted **one round at a time**, Pokhar tola first and the Bazaar
last. Each round shows on a TV-style board, the first time you ever see exact numbers. Workers' faces
crowd the mesh and phones ring.

The shapes it can take:

| Shape              | How it feels                                                                                                                        |
| ------------------ | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Landslide**      | You lead from round one. Workers start garlanding you by round three.                                                               |
| **Comeback**       | Behind after Pokhar tola and Paschim tola (the rival's strongholds), ahead after Purvi tola, clinging on through the Bazaar.        |
| **Collapse**       | Ahead early, then the tola you neglected counts last and wipes out your lead.                                                       |
| **Narrow loss**    | Within 50 votes. Pintu, quietly: _"Naya tola…"_                                                                                     |
| **Tie** [new rule] | Exactly equal. As in real panchayat rules, the winner is decided by **draw of lots**. The returning officer's hand goes into a box. |

---

## 7. The end card

A newsprint card, stamped, designed to be shared:

- **Result and margin,** in exact numbers at last.
- **What decided it:** the top 3 choices by their effect on votes, e.g. _"Fixing the Purvi tola
  handpump on day 4"_, _"Expelling your worker on day 9"_, _"Rajendra Babu's envelopes in Naya
  tola"_.
- **Your ledger:** promises made, kept, broken and still open; favours owed; notices and FIRs.
- **A title,** and one closing paragraph in the voice of the local paper.

### MVP endings

| Title                                | When                                                                         |
| ------------------------------------ | ---------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **The People's Sarpanch**            | Won; kept most campaign promises; low legal trouble; no favours owed         |
| **The Giant-Killer**                 | Won from far behind against a strong rival                                   |
| **The Promise Machine**              | Won, but with many open or broken promises (_"They'll be back to collect."_) |
| **The Bought Chair**                 | Won with the donor's money or cash for votes; favours still owed             |
| **The Divider**                      | Won or lost after the divisive path; EC notice on file                       |
| **Won by Lottery / Lost by Lottery** | A tie, decided by draw of lots                                               |
| **The Nearly-Man / Nearly-Woman**    | Lost by under 3%                                                             |
| **The Outsider Who Stayed Outside**  | Lost heavily                                                                 |
| **Barred**                           | Disqualified before polling day                                              |

Endings combine: you can be _The Giant-Killer_ **and** owe Mahesh-ji a road contract. The card
shows both, because that is the theme: _what did power cost you?_

## 8. Five sample games

**1. The honest newcomer.**

- Visits every tola in week 1, fixes the handpump on day 4, refuses Mahesh-ji, expels the worker.
- Gives a specific rally speech on water and jobs, keeps 3 of 4 campaign promises.
- Behind until round 3, wins by 140 votes.
- **Ending:** _The People's Sarpanch._ What decided it: the handpump, Sunita didi spreading the word,
  the honest interview.

**2. The promise machine.**

- Promises roads, jobs, pensions and a college in the first rally. Trust surges.
- Week 3: six deadlines pass and two are kept. Priya's video goes around. Elders drift back to
  Rajendra Babu.
- Wins narrowly on youth turnout, or loses narrowly; it's a coin flip by design.
- **Ending:** _The Promise Machine,_ with 9 open promises ("they'll be back").

**3. The bought chair.**

- Accepts the donor, rallies twice, and pays out on the last night in Naya tola and the Bazaar.
- Legal trouble climbs to 64 and an FIR is filed on day 19. Wins by 60.
- **Ending:** _The Bought Chair:_ owes Mahesh-ji the road contract; FIR pending. (At tier 2 this
  comes back.)

**4. The divider.**

- Gives a speech blaming one community. One group consolidates.
- An EC notice follows. Other groups turn and never return past lukewarm. Some of the speaker's own
  women supporters stay home.
- Usually loses. When it wins, the card says so plainly: _The Divider,_ with a communal tension
  event and a notice on file.

**5. The idle one.**

- Tears the calendar most mornings and writes two statements all game.
- The dynasty's head start holds. Loses by 30%.
- **Ending:** _The Outsider Who Stayed Outside._ What decided it: Rajendra Babu's sweets, and the
  handpump nobody fixed.

**Edge cases the game handles**

- **Writing only in Hindi (Devanagari) or Hinglish:** understood the same way. Pintu and the
  villagers reply in the language you used.
- **Writing nonsense or trying to instruct the AI:** no effect, with a confused crowd, and Pintu
  saying people didn't understand.
- **Offline / AI down:** the game keeps working with keyword understanding and stock lines. Pintu:
  _"Log abhi soch rahe hain."_ (People are still thinking.)

---

## 9. After the end card

- **MVP:** the run ends there. **Play again** starts a new campaign with a new seed: different
  petitioners, different events, a rival who reacts differently.
- **Later tiers:** winning unlocks tier 2 (Zila Parishad / ward), and the bahi-khata carries forward,
  favours included: Mahesh-ji calls to collect. Losing sends you to _the wilderness_: rebuild, try a
  smaller seat, or back someone else as kingmaker.

## 10. Rules this walk-through adds

Folded into `ENGINE.md` §15:

1. **New-game form:** name and form of address (_bhaiya/didi_); every voice uses it.
2. **Statement #1:** the "why are you standing" line on day 0, free (no slot), stored and quotable;
   only a tiny reputation nudge.
3. **Silence period:** day 21 allows only door-to-door visits, public works and the phone.
4. **The last night:** a scripted temptation on day 21 (the rival is buying votes; match or abstain).
5. **Polling day:** day 22, no actions; turnout events and calls.
6. **Tie rule:** an exact tie is decided by draw of lots (seeded RNG, shown on screen).
7. **Endings stack:** the end card shows one main title plus any "cost" tags (favours owed, FIR,
   notice, broken promises).
8. **Settings:** a stamp on the title screen, with AI model selection per task (see `ENGINE.md` §11.1).
